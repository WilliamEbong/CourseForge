# Section 5 — Graded assessment

## Assessment design

The bank contains **15 items**. Every objective is assessed at least twice, and most items require application, analysis, or evaluation rather than isolated recall. Items are written so they can be presented in random order without depending on earlier graded items. A suggested passing range is **75–80%** for this independent awareness/application course, subject to the implementing organization’s instructional policy. Passing does **not** demonstrate professional certification, legal competence, industrial-hygiene qualification, investigator qualification, or authorization to perform hazardous work.

## Appendix A — Glossary

| Term | Plain-language definition | Citation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Administrative control | A rule, procedure, schedule, training requirement, access restriction, inspection, or other work-practice control. Administrative controls are lower in the hierarchy than elimination and engineering controls where higher-order controls are reasonably practicable. | [AB-OHS-2 s.9] |
| As low as reasonably achievable (ALARA) | The Alberta Part 4 principle that exposure to harmful substances is to be kept as low as reasonably achievable, in addition to applicable exposure-limit requirements. | [AB-OHS-4 s.16] |
| CAPA | Corrective and preventive action. A formal quality-system concept in some regulated sectors and a useful management framework more broadly; it is not automatically a universal legal requirement for every laboratory. | [HC-CAPA; HC-GMP-V4] |
| Containment | Immediate or interim action used to limit spread, exposure, or impact while a problem is being investigated. | [HC-CAPA] |
| Consequence / severity | The credible magnitude of harm if an exposure or event occurs. | [AB-OHS-2; source dossier §3.4] |
| Contributing factor | A condition or action that increased the likelihood or severity of an event but may not explain the event by itself. | [CCOHS-INVEST] |
| Correction | Action that fixes the immediate detected problem. | [HC-CAPA] |
| Corrective action | Action intended to address a cause of a detected problem so recurrence is less likely. | [HC-CAPA] |
| Deviation | A departure from an approved or expected process, procedure, method, condition, or requirement. Exact definitions vary by quality system. | [HC-CAPA] |
| Effectiveness check | A planned review that asks whether an implemented action actually produced the intended improvement. | [HC-CAPA] |
| Engineering control | A physical or designed control that removes, isolates, or reduces exposure to a hazard. | [AB-OHS-2 s.9] |
| Exposure | Contact between a person and a harmful agent through a relevant route such as inhalation, skin, eye, ingestion, or injection. | [AB-OHS-4; CCOHS-SDS] |
| Globally Harmonized System (GHS) | The United Nations international model for chemical hazard classification and communication. Canadian WHMIS uses GHS-based concepts through Canadian law. | [UNECE-GHS11; HC-WHMIS-GUIDE] |
| Hazard | A source, material, situation, or condition with the potential to cause harm. | [AB-OHS-2] |
| Hierarchy of controls | A structured approach to hazard control that prioritizes removal and higher-order controls before relying mainly on administrative controls and PPE. Alberta’s legal control sequence is set out in OHS Code s.9. | [AB-OHS-2 s.9] |
| Incident | An undesired event that caused or could have caused harm or loss. Internal organizational definitions can vary. | [CCOHS-INVEST] |
| Likelihood | The chance that an exposure or event will occur under the defined conditions. | [AB-OHS-2; source dossier §3.5] |
| Near miss | An event in which serious harm did not occur but could credibly have occurred. Internal terminology does not replace Alberta’s statutory tests for reporting or investigation. | [AB-OHS-ACT-33] |
| Nonconformity | Failure to meet a requirement, such as a law, procedure, specification, standard, or quality-system requirement. | [HC-CAPA; WHO-LQMS] |
| Occupational exposure limit (OEL) | A regulated benchmark used in controlling occupational airborne exposure. Applying substance-specific limits can require competent occupational-hygiene expertise. | [AB-OHS-4 ss.16–20] |
| Personal protective equipment (PPE) | Equipment worn by a worker to reduce exposure or injury. PPE is a lower-order control than elimination and engineering where higher-order controls are reasonably practicable. | [AB-OHS-2 s.9] |
| Preventive action | Action intended to prevent a potential or related problem before occurrence or recurrence elsewhere. Terminology varies by management system. | [HC-CAPA] |
| Residual risk | A plain-language course term for risk remaining after existing controls are considered. It is not presented as a statutory Alberta definition. | [source dossier §8.8] |
| Risk | A judgment about the likelihood and consequence of harm under the actual task and control conditions. | [AB-OHS-2] |
| Root cause | An underlying causal factor whose effective control should materially reduce recurrence. Complex incidents can have multiple interacting causes. | [CCOHS-INVEST] |
| Safety Data Sheet (SDS) | A standardized hazardous-product information document. It supports hazard communication but is not a complete task-specific risk assessment. | [CCOHS-SDS] |
| Substitution | Replacing a hazard or process with a less hazardous alternative, followed by reassessment of the resulting risk. | [AB-OHS-2 s.9; source dossier §9.2] |
| WHMIS | Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System, Canada’s workplace hazardous-product communication system. | [HC-WHMIS-RR] |
| Work-site label | The Alberta workplace label used in specified circumstances for hazardous products, including many decanted products. | [AB-OHS-29 ss.398, 400] |

## Appendix G — Quality-control notes

### G1. Unresolved evidence gaps

No evidence gap prevents delivery of the intended awareness/application course. The following are intentionally **not resolved by the course** because they require local or specialist judgment rather than generic instruction:

- substance- and task-specific glove selection or breakthrough time;
- respirator need, type, cartridge, fit, or respiratory-protection program decisions;
- chemical-specific OEL application, combined-exposure calculations, extended-shift adjustment, or sampling strategy;
- medical evaluation after exposure;
- universal spill-response quantities or neutralization methods;
- pyrophoric, water-reactive, peroxide-former, or other specialized high-hazard handling procedures;
- reactive-chemistry scale-up engineering;
- chemical-waste disposal routes;
- fire-code quantity limits;
- legal conclusions about reportability in ambiguous fact patterns.

These exclusions are intentional scope controls, not missing learner content. [AB-OHS-4; AB-OHS-ACT-33; source dossier §26]

### G2. Research-versus-blueprint conflicts

No material factual conflict between the expanded research dossier and the approved instructional-design blueprint was identified during storyboard production. The storyboard preserves the blueprint’s two-hour target by keeping detailed exposure calculations, small-container variations, specialized hazard-family detail, formal code-of-practice thresholds, exact investigation-report distribution, ISO/WHO context, and deeper method analysis in optional or reference layers rather than deleting them.

### G3. Content requiring review before publication

**Alberta legal/regulatory review:** Confirm current wording and applicability of OHS Act s.33 and OHS Code Parts 2, 4, 7, and 29 immediately before publication.

**Chemical-safety / occupational-hygiene review:** Review task-risk, ventilation, OEL, emergency-washing, storage, and specialist-boundary language for consistency with the implementing organization’s program.

**Quality/regulatory review:** If the course will be used in a regulated pharmaceutical, natural-health-product, medical-device, diagnostic, or manufacturing environment, confirm that the organization’s CAPA/deviation terminology and sector-specific requirements are added separately rather than inferred from the general course.

**Emergency-response review:** Replace generic escalation prompts with the organization’s actual emergency contacts/procedures only after local competent review.

**Accessibility review:** Validate keyboard order, table semantics, focus state, text alternatives, contrast, and non-colour dependency in the final implementation.

1. **Alberta OHS Code:** the research identified a Code version in force 31 March 2025 and active 2025–2026 review topics. Recheck the official Alberta publication and current amendments, especially Part 4/Schedule 1 and technical-standard references. [AB-CODE-STATUS; AB-CODE-REVIEW]
