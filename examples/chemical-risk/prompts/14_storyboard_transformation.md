# Shared Prompt 14 LMS Neutral Course Storyboard

Paste this prompt into each course chat after reviewing its instructional-design blueprint.

```text
Using the cited research dossier and the instructional-design blueprint already produced in this conversation, create the complete, author-ready course storyboard.

This is the final transformation stage. The result must follow the mature instructional architecture described below while remaining neutral enough to build in any learning management system or e-learning authoring environment.

SOURCE-OF-TRUTH ORDER

1. The cited research dossier is the factual source of truth.
2. The approved instructional-design blueprint controls audience, scope, sequence, objectives, and assessment alignment.
3. This prompt controls the storyboard format.

If the blueprint accidentally conflicts with the research evidence, preserve the more precise researched claim and flag the conflict in the final quality-control notes. Do not resolve factual conflicts by guessing.

NON-NEGOTIABLE ACCURACY AND CITATION RULES

1. Preserve the meaning, qualifications, jurisdiction, dates, versions, uncertainty, and citation relationships from the research dossier.
2. Keep citations in the same content block as the claims they support. Do not rely only on an end bibliography.
3. Assessment rationales and scenario feedback must also include the citations supporting the correct answer.
4. Do not invent facts, statistics, quotations, regulatory requirements, standards clauses, incidents, organizations, outcomes, or citations.
5. Do not insert uncited general-knowledge statements when they are substantive to the course.
6. If essential information is missing, mark a concise evidence gap in the quality-control notes instead of filling it from memory.
7. Do not present an independent portfolio course as certification, official regulator training, professional authorization, or employer-specific instruction.
8. Preserve the course's safety and scope exclusions. Do not convert conceptual content into hazardous, clinical, engineering, legal, or regulated operating instructions.

LMS-NEUTRAL DESIGN RULES

- Use the terms “content block,” “screen,” “page,” “interaction,” and “assessment item” generically.
- Do not mention or optimize for Articulate Storyline, Rise, Moodle, Canvas, SCORM, xAPI, or another specific platform unless the research subject itself requires mentioning one.
- Do not include platform navigation lessons, template-maintenance instructions, developer housekeeping, grey instructional rows, or statements such as “to be determined by developers.”
- Do not depend on hover, rollover, drag-and-drop, colour, animation, audio, or a mouse as the only way to learn or respond.
- For every interaction or visual, provide an accessible text or keyboard-operable alternative.
- Describe what a visual must teach; do not request decorative stock imagery.
- Use one clear instructional purpose per content block. Prefer concise learner-facing chunks, usually about 40 to 100 words, but split or expand based on comprehension rather than an arbitrary platform limit.
- Never delete necessary qualifications or evidence merely to shorten a screen.

STORYBOARD TABLE FORMAT

Build the main storyboard as a Markdown table or a series of consistently structured tables with these columns:

1. Block ID
2. Type
3. Format or subtype
4. Screen or block title
5. Complete learner-facing content
6. Learning treatment and interaction behaviour
7. Visual or media direction
8. Accessibility and text alternative
9. Learning-objective mapping
10. Source citations

Use types such as:

- section title;
- topic title;
- content;
- example;
- comparison;
- process;
- scenario;
- interaction;
- formative self-assessment;
- course review;
- graded assessment;
- glossary;
- references.

Write the actual learner-facing content. Do not return a high-level outline, production brief, or list of topics to be written later.

REQUIRED COURSE ARCHITECTURE

COURSE METADATA

Begin with a concise metadata table containing:

- course title;
- course level;
- intended audience;
- assumed prior knowledge;
- recommended prerequisites or optional preparatory resources;
- estimated seat time;
- course description;
- four to six measurable learning objectives;
- explicit scope and qualification boundaries.

SECTION 1 COURSE INTRODUCTION

Include:

- title block;
- why the subject matters to this audience;
- concise course description and scope;
- what learners will and will not be able to do after completing it;
- learning objectives;
- a short orientation to any continuing scenario or case used in the course.

Do not include generic platform-navigation instructions, “click Next” language, course-structure rollovers, or authoring-system housekeeping. These are the platform-specific grey rows intentionally removed from the source storyboard architecture.

SECTION 2 BACKGROUND AND FOUNDATIONS

Include only the background learners actually need before the main content:

- prerequisite concepts;
- essential terminology;
- a high-level system, lifecycle, or process model;
- relevant jurisdictional, regulatory, standards, ethical, or organizational context;
- common misconceptions that would interfere with later learning;
- optional preparatory or deeper-reading resources clearly separated from required content.

Move from familiar concepts to unfamiliar ones. Define every acronym on first use.

SECTION 3 COURSE CONTENT

Organize the substantive course into clearly titled topics or modules. Each topic should normally include:

1. a topic title and relevance statement;
2. the necessary concept or process explanation;
3. an example, comparison, diagram, or worked scenario where it improves comprehension;
4. practical implications, decision points, or escalation boundaries appropriate to the audience;
5. a brief synthesis;
6. two to four formative self-assessment items or one substantial scenario-based activity.

When the course compares methods, technologies, controls, or approaches, use the following repeated analysis pattern where it genuinely fits:

- what it is;
- how it works at the intended level;
- where or when it may be used;
- the method, lifecycle stage, or objective it supports;
- benefits or strengths;
- limitations and uncertainty;
- risks, dependencies, and implementation considerations;
- effectiveness or suitability relative to alternatives;
- conditions requiring specialist judgment.

Do not force this pattern onto material where it would distort the subject.

FORMATIVE SELF-ASSESSMENTS

For every formative item provide:

- item ID;
- item type;
- objective mapping;
- complete question or scenario;
- response options or expected response;
- correct answer;
- feedback for the correct response;
- explanatory feedback for each incorrect response where applicable;
- supporting citation or citations;
- accessible alternative if the proposed interaction is not natively accessible.

Use a purposeful mixture of multiple choice, multiple response, matching, categorization, sequencing, interpretation, and scenario-based judgment. Avoid trick questions, ambiguous stems, “all of the above,” implausible distractors, and trivia unsupported by an objective.

SECTION 4 COURSE REVIEW

Include:

- a review organized by learning objective rather than a simple repetition of headings;
- the central mental model, lifecycle, or decision process;
- the most important distinctions, limitations, and escalation boundaries;
- a short integrated scenario or decision summary where appropriate;
- key terms and acronyms;
- a complete linked reference list.

Each objective review should state what the learner should now be able to explain, distinguish, interpret, compare, select, document, or escalate.

SECTION 5 GRADED ASSESSMENT

Create an assessment bank of approximately 12 to 15 items unless the blueprint justifies a different number. Ensure:

- every learning objective is assessed by at least two items across the bank;
- item difficulty and cognitive demand match the objective;
- scenario and interpretation items are used when performance matters more than recall;
- questions can be presented in random order without losing necessary context;
- unexplained acronyms are not used;
- formative questions are not copied word for word into the graded assessment;
- no question depends on inaccessible interaction mechanics.

For every graded item provide:

- assessment item ID;
- item type;
- objective mapping;
- complete stem or scenario;
- answer options or expected response;
- correct answer;
- rationale;
- rationale for incorrect options where useful;
- supporting citation or citations;
- difficulty level: foundational, applied, or integrative.

Recommend a passing-score range only as an instructional-design suggestion. Do not imply that passing demonstrates professional certification or legal competence.

APPENDICES

After the storyboard, include:

A. Glossary
- plain-language definitions;
- citations for factual or formal definitions.

B. Acronym list
- acronym;
- full term;
- first-use Block ID.

C. Complete references
- retain the citation identifiers used throughout;
- include author or issuing organization, title, date, version or edition, URL or DOI, and access date where available;
- separate authoritative primary sources, standards or frameworks, peer-reviewed evidence, and secondary/contextual sources.

D. Visual and media inventory
- visual ID;
- storyboard Block ID;
- learning purpose;
- content that must be shown;
- source or data citation;
- accessibility text requirement;
- note whether an original diagram should be created rather than reproducing a protected figure.

E. Objective and assessment traceability matrix
- learning objective;
- teaching Block IDs;
- formative-practice IDs;
- graded-assessment IDs.

F. Citation audit
- source identifier;
- all Block IDs using it;
- claim or purpose supported;
- any limitation, jurisdiction, date, or version that must remain visible.

G. Quality-control notes
- unresolved evidence gaps;
- conflicts between the research and blueprint;
- content requiring subject-matter-expert, legal, regulatory, clinical, engineering, safety, privacy, or community review;
- time-sensitive information that must be rechecked before publication.

FINAL QUALITY CHECK

Before returning the storyboard, verify that:

- the course follows the five-section architecture;
- all learner-facing content is actually written;
- every factual content block retains nearby citations;
- every objective is taught, practised, reviewed, and assessed;
- assessment answers and rationales are complete;
- prerequisite context is sufficient for an educated nontechnical learner;
- the language is approximately high-school reading level without sacrificing accuracy;
- no platform-specific grey rows or navigation instructions remain;
- interactions have accessible alternatives;
- references, glossary terms, acronyms, visuals, and assessments are traceable to the relevant blocks;
- no placeholders, fabricated facts, or unsupported claims remain.

Return the complete course storyboard in Markdown. Do not summarize it or replace any required section with “continue as above.”
```
