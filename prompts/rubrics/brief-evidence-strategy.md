# Rubric: brief-evidence-strategy

**Stage:** RESEARCH_BRIEF. **Lens:** will the planned evidence be authoritative, current, correctly typed and pinpoint-citable?

## Read

1. The research brief: `sourceHierarchy`, `jurisdictions`, `evidenceRequirements`, `currentnessRequirements`, section notes.
2. The concept brief for subject, jurisdiction and risk tier.

## Checks

- [ ] **Hierarchy fits the subject.** Legal and regulatory subjects rank legislation and regulation above government guidance, then standards and professional guidance; scientific subjects rank peer-reviewed evidence and authoritative bodies first. News, vendor and tertiary sources are low and constrained.
- [ ] **Rationales constrain use.** Each hierarchy entry says what that source type may and may not support (for example "standards: describe what the standard specifies, never present as law unless incorporated by legislation").
- [ ] **Law vs guidance vs standard.** The brief requires the dossier to keep these distinct, and section notes flag topics where confusion is likely.
- [ ] **Pinpoint citation.** Evidence requirements demand section, clause, page or table locators for legal and technical claims.
- [ ] **Statistics.** Requirements demand the dataset or report, year and population for any number.
- [ ] **Currentness.** Time-sensitive material is named (consolidated legislation, standard editions, guidance versions, statistics), and researchers must record version, consolidation or effective date, and access date.
- [ ] **Jurisdictions complete.** Every jurisdiction whose rules apply is listed, including federal/provincial or EU/member-state layers where duties are split.
- [ ] **Conflicting sources.** The brief says how to handle disagreement between sources (report both, prefer the more authoritative, never average).
- [ ] **No reliance on inaccessible sources without a plan.** If key standards are paywalled, the brief says how they may be described (catalogue entries, official summaries) without inventing clause content.

## Severity calibration

- `critical`: the hierarchy would let guidance or a voluntary standard be treated as a legal requirement in a regulated subject; no currentness requirement for law that is regularly amended.
- `major`: missing jurisdiction layer; no locator requirement for legal claims; no rule for statistics; tertiary sources allowed to support core claims.
- `minor`: rationales that are generic; a currentness item missing for low-impact material.
- `style`: wording.

## Not your job

Whether every learning goal has a question (brief-scope-completeness); hazardous-content boundaries (brief-safety).
