# Rubric: audience-cognitive-load

**Stage:** INSTRUCTIONAL_DESIGN. **Lens:** can the stated audience actually learn this, in this order and amount, without drowning or being patronised?

## Read

1. The design's `audience`, `prerequisites`, modules (content sequence, misconceptions, examples, durations) and glossary plan.
2. The concept brief's audience description.

## Checks

- [ ] **Prerequisites realistic.** Assumed prior knowledge matches the audience. Specialist concepts are not assumed for a non-specialist audience.
- [ ] **Vocabulary load.** New terms per module are manageable (roughly 3–6 genuinely new terms), each planned for definition before use; acronyms are introduced deliberately.
- [ ] **Chunking.** Each content point carries one idea or decision; modules do not stack several unfamiliar models back to back without practice between them.
- [ ] **Progressive disclosure.** Essential explanation is separated from optional depth, and optional depth is not required for the graded assessment.
- [ ] **Mental model first.** A simple overall model or process appears before details that hang off it.
- [ ] **Misconceptions.** Each module anticipates the misconceptions this audience is likely to bring, and plans to address them directly.
- [ ] **Examples fit the audience's world.** Examples come from the learners' work context and are concrete; analogies are accurate and followed by the literal explanation.
- [ ] **Pacing.** Module durations match their content load; dense regulatory material is broken up with application.
- [ ] **Tone and respect.** The design treats learners as capable adults; plain language without condescension.
- [ ] **Reading level plan.** The design supports roughly Grade 9–11 learner-facing language without dropping precision.

## Severity calibration

- `critical`: the core objectives depend on prerequisite knowledge the audience does not have and the design never teaches.
- `major`: a module introducing many new terms or models at once with no practice; optional depth required for assessment; no misconceptions identified for a topic where they are well known.
- `minor`: a better example or analogy available; an acronym planned without a first-use definition.
- `style`: tone preferences.

## Not your job

Whether content is correct (source-fidelity), mapped (alignment), or sequenced by subject logic (learning-architecture).

## Categories

`cognitive-load`, `instructional`
