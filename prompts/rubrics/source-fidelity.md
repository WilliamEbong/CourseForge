# Rubric: source-fidelity

**Stage:** INSTRUCTIONAL_DESIGN. **Lens:** did turning research into a design preserve what the evidence says, its qualifications, and its links to sources?

## Read

1. The design: objectives (`claimIds`, `sourceIds`), module `contentSequence`, `examples`, `glossaryPlan`, `dispositions`, `evidenceGaps`.
2. The claim ledger and dossier text for every claim the design cites.

## Checks

- [ ] **Faithful paraphrase.** Content points that summarise claims keep their meaning: same modality, same scope, same jurisdiction, same numbers.
- [ ] **Qualifications preserved.** Exceptions, thresholds, "reasonably practicable", population limits and dates survive the simplification.
- [ ] **Law vs guidance vs standard.** Guidance and voluntary standards are not planned to be taught as legal requirements; emerging evidence is not planned as settled fact.
- [ ] **Correct claim links.** The claim IDs attached to an objective or content point actually support it; combined statements cite every claim needed.
- [ ] **No new facts.** Examples, glossary definitions and content points introduce no uncited factual claims. Gap-filling facts are recorded in `evidenceGaps` with their source, not slipped in.
- [ ] **Authoritative sources retained.** Simplification has not replaced a primary source with a weaker secondary one.
- [ ] **Evidence gaps honest.** Weakly supported areas are recorded as gaps with a sensible action, not taught as certain.
- [ ] **Invented scenarios labelled.** Illustrative scenarios are clearly hypothetical; no invented incidents, organisations or statistics presented as real.

Evidence must quote the design text and the claim text it distorts, with IDs.

## Severity calibration

- `critical`: a content point that reverses or materially changes a claim; guidance planned as law; an invented fact or statistic.
- `major`: a dropped qualification; a wrong claim link on a core point; a weakly supported topic planned as core without a recorded gap.
- `minor`: a missing claim ID on an enrichment point; imprecise paraphrase that does not change meaning.
- `style`: wording.

## Not your job

Whether the dossier itself is right (research reviewers already covered it); alignment and assessment design.

## Categories

`evidence`, `citation`, `accuracy`
