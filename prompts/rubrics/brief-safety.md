# Rubric: brief-safety

**Stage:** RESEARCH_BRIEF. **Lens:** does the brief keep research, and therefore the course, inside a safe awareness-level boundary?

## Read

1. The research brief: `safetyBoundaries`, `exclusions`, `scope`, research questions, section notes.
2. The concept brief: risk tier, out-of-scope list, high-stakes assumptions.

## Checks

- [ ] **Boundaries are specific.** Safety boundaries name what must not be turned into instructions (quantities, concentrations, reaction or process conditions, step-by-step hazardous procedures, dosing, bypassing controls, exploit steps, individual legal or medical advice). "Be safe" is not a boundary.
- [ ] **Questions do not invite operational detail.** Research questions ask about concepts, responsibilities, decision criteria and escalation, not how to perform hazardous tasks. Flag any question whose honest answer is a procedure the audience is not qualified to perform.
- [ ] **Professional boundaries.** The brief states what the course will not qualify learners to do, and requires the dossier to identify where specialist judgement is needed (occupational hygienist, clinician, lawyer, engineer).
- [ ] **Certification claims.** Nothing implies the course is certification, official regulator training or professional authorisation.
- [ ] **Concept exclusions carried forward.** Every out-of-scope item and high-stakes assumption from the concept is respected.
- [ ] **Dual-use and sensitive material.** Where sources contain hazardous operational content, the brief instructs researchers to summarise the concept and cite, not reproduce.
- [ ] **Vulnerable contexts.** If the subject touches self-harm, abuse, medical emergencies or similar, the brief requires responsible framing and signposting to appropriate help rather than instructions.

## Severity calibration

- `blocker`: a research question or section that directs researchers to collect hazardous operational instructions for a lay audience.
- `critical`: no safety boundaries at all for a high-stakes subject; an exclusion from the concept silently dropped.
- `major`: boundaries too vague to apply; no statement of what the course does not qualify learners to do.
- `minor`: a boundary that could be worded more precisely.
- `style`: wording.

## Not your job

Source quality (brief-evidence-strategy) and coverage of learning goals (brief-scope-completeness).
