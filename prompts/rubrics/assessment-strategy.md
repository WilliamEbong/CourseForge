# Rubric: assessment-strategy

**Stage:** INSTRUCTIONAL_DESIGN. **Lens:** will the planned practice and graded assessment produce valid evidence that learners met the objectives?

## Read

1. The design's `alignment`, `assessmentStrategy`, module `formativePractice`, and `scenarioStrategy`.
2. The objectives and their Bloom levels.

## Checks

- [ ] **Coverage.** The graded item count allows at least two items per objective, with more for the most important objectives.
- [ ] **Level match.** Planned item types can reach each objective's level: scenario, interpretation, classification and decision items for apply/analyse/evaluate; recall only where the objective is recall.
- [ ] **Item-type mix.** A purposeful mix (single choice, multiple response, matching, categorisation, sequencing, scenario judgement) chosen for what each objective requires, not for novelty.
- [ ] **Formative before graded.** Every objective gets formative practice with feedback before it is graded; formative items will not be copied into the graded bank.
- [ ] **Difficulty spread.** The plan includes foundational, applied and integrative items, weighted towards applied for workplace objectives.
- [ ] **Passing score.** The passing percent is a sensible design suggestion and is not framed as certification or legal competence.
- [ ] **Feedback intent.** The strategy requires explanatory feedback and rationales with citations.
- [ ] **Accessibility.** No planned item type depends on drag, hover, colour, audio or timing as the only way to respond.
- [ ] **Randomisation readiness.** Graded items are planned to stand alone, without relying on earlier items.

## Severity calibration

- `critical`: a graded plan that cannot measure a core objective at all; assessment only by recall for a course whose objectives are decisions.
- `major`: fewer than two graded items planned for an objective; no formative practice for an objective; planned inaccessible item mechanics.
- `minor`: an unbalanced difficulty spread; a passing score without rationale.
- `style`: naming.

## Not your job

Whether objectives are well written and taught (alignment); item wording (storyboard assessment reviewer).

## Categories

`assessment`, `alignment`
