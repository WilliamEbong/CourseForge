# Rubric: safety-scope

**Stage:** RESEARCH_DOSSIER. **Lens:** does the dossier stay inside the brief's safety boundaries and scope, and does it make specialist boundaries visible?

## Read

1. The brief's `safetyBoundaries`, `exclusions` and `scope`; the concept's risk tier and out-of-scope list.
2. Every dossier section, with attention to procedures, quantities, and any "how to" language.

## Checks

- [ ] **No operational hazard content.** No quantities, concentrations, reaction or process conditions, step-by-step hazardous procedures, dosing, instructions for defeating controls, or exploit steps, beyond what an awareness-level audience needs to recognise a situation and escalate.
- [ ] **Escalation boundaries.** Where a decision requires a specialist (occupational hygienist, clinician, engineer, lawyer, emergency services), the dossier says so explicitly.
- [ ] **No authorisation implied.** Nothing suggests that reading the material qualifies someone to perform regulated or hazardous work.
- [ ] **Legal and medical advice.** Content explains principles and duties; it does not tell an individual what to do in their specific legal or clinical situation.
- [ ] **Scope drift.** Sections do not wander into excluded topics, or into depth that belongs to a different course.
- [ ] **Sensitive material.** Distressing topics (injury, fatality, abuse) are framed factually and respectfully; incident descriptions are not gratuitous.
- [ ] **Hazardous sources.** Where a cited source contains operational detail, the dossier summarises the concept rather than reproducing the procedure.

## Severity calibration

- `blocker`: step-by-step hazardous instructions or dangerous quantities that a lay learner could act on.
- `critical`: content implying learners may perform regulated or specialist work; missing escalation boundary on a high-risk decision.
- `major`: operational detail beyond awareness level that is not dangerous in itself but will pull the course out of scope; excluded topic researched at length.
- `minor`: a boundary that could be stated more clearly.
- `style`: tone.

## Not your job

Accuracy of claims (evidence-source), coverage (domain-completeness), currentness.

## Categories

`safety`, `scope`
