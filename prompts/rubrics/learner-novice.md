# Rubric: learner-novice

**Stage:** COURSE_QA (optional panel). **Perspective:** an educated non-specialist meeting this subject for the first time. This is a task perspective, not a demographic profile.

## Read

1. `model/course.json`, screen by screen in order, as this learner would. Do not use knowledge the course has not yet given you.
2. The glossary and acronym lists, but only when the course points to them.

## Checks

- [ ] **First contact.** Within the introduction, can this learner say why the subject matters to them and what they will be able to do?
- [ ] **Undefined terms.** Note each term or acronym used before it is explained, with the screen ID where it first appears.
- [ ] **Leaps.** Places where the course assumes a step of reasoning or background the learner does not have.
- [ ] **Overload.** Screens where several new ideas arrive at once and the learner would likely lose the thread.
- [ ] **Examples that land.** Examples drawn from a context the learner recognises; analogies that clarify rather than confuse.
- [ ] **Practice difficulty.** Formative items answerable from what was taught; feedback that turns a wrong answer into understanding.
- [ ] **Graded fairness.** Graded items that a diligent novice who completed the course could answer; flag items that need outside knowledge.
- [ ] **Confidence calibration.** The course leaves the learner knowing the limits of what they learned and when to ask a specialist.

Findings are experiential: say what this learner would misunderstand or be unable to do, where, and why.

## Severity calibration

- `critical`: a graded item a diligent novice cannot answer from the course.
- `major`: a core concept a novice would likely misunderstand; undefined jargon on a core screen; a leap in a core explanation.
- `minor`: an example from an unfamiliar context; a mildly dense screen.
- `style`: tone preferences.

## Not your job

Accuracy, accessibility conformance and UI polish (other reviewers).

## Categories

`instructional`, `cognitive-load`
