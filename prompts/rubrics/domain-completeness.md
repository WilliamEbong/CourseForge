# Rubric: domain-completeness

**Stage:** RESEARCH_DOSSIER. **Lens:** does the dossier answer every planned question with enough depth for accurate teaching, and has it missed anything a domain expert would expect?

## Read

1. The research brief: research questions, priorities, dossier plan and section notes.
2. The dossier sections and the claim ledger.
3. Use web tools only to confirm that a suspected omission is a real, well-established part of the domain.

## Checks

- [ ] **Question coverage.** Every `core` question is answered directly in its assigned section, not just mentioned. Supporting questions are answered or explicitly noted as not found.
- [ ] **Depth.** Answers go beyond headings: they explain mechanisms, responsibilities, conditions and exceptions at the precision the course needs.
- [ ] **Expert expectations.** Topics any competent practitioner would expect in a course on this subject are present or explicitly excluded by the brief (for example SDS limitations in a chemical-risk course; effectiveness checks in a CAPA course).
- [ ] **Definitions.** Every key term has a definition claim with a source.
- [ ] **Limitations and misconceptions.** The dossier records what is uncertain, contested or commonly misunderstood.
- [ ] **Section seams.** Parallel sections do not leave a gap between them (a topic each assumed the other covered) or contradict each other.
- [ ] **Open questions.** Each section's "limitations and open questions" is specific and honest; gaps that affect core teaching are visible.
- [ ] **Balance.** One perspective or source does not dominate where the field has recognised alternatives.

## Severity calibration

- `critical`: a core question unanswered or answered so thinly that the course would have to teach from memory; two sections that contradict each other on a core point.
- `major`: an expected domain topic missing without exclusion; a definition missing for a key term; a seam gap between sections.
- `minor`: a supporting question answered briefly; a useful example or distinction missing.
- `style`: organisation within a section.

## Not your job

Whether the cited sources support each claim (evidence-source), currentness of versions (currentness-jurisdiction), hazardous detail (safety-scope).

## Categories

`completeness`, `scope`
